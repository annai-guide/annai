Phase 2 (search, languages, pops and screens) was released as build 86c07916891b on 2026-10-23. Several of its fixes can only be confirmed on a real phone; this list is those checks, plus the car, the lock screen, the share target and Android's Doze, which you asked for earlier. The iPhone Air is the main phone; an Android phone where a line says so.

Each item says what to do, what should happen, and the Trace lines to look at. The Trace is in the menu (Trace); "Kind **Models**, Type contains `live.reply`" means: pick Models under Kind, and type `live.reply` in "Type contains". Tap a Trace line to see everything in it. The areas run from quick and important to slow: pops and languages need ten minutes at a desk; Doze needs an idle hour, and the car needs a drive.

## Pops in her realtime voice

The investigation (`.superpowers/evidence/pops/REPORT.md`) found three causes of clicks: she was stopped in one frame with no fade; she started with almost nothing buffered, so a late piece left a hole with sharp edges; and each piece was resampled on its own. Her live voice now plays through one AudioWorklet player that resamples across pieces, waits for 150 ms of sound before it starts (250 ms at most), fades over 5 ms when it runs dry and over 12 ms when stopped, and reports what it did. Only an ear on a real phone can confirm the clicks are gone.

Set up: voice on, the chip saying **Voice on · Realtime**, a quiet room, the phone's own speaker first (then AirPods for P3 and P8).

- [ ] **P1. The sound rate.** Tap Shiori and ask anything. Trace → Kind **Models**, Type contains `live.session`: the "ready" line reads "live session ready · Gemini 3.8 Live · … s · sound at 48.0 kHz, … ms processing latency". On Android Chrome it also has "… ms output latency"; Safari has none, so the iPhone line has no output latency. Write down the rate and the latencies. A rate of 44.1 kHz is worth noting: the old crackle was worst there.
- [ ] **P2. Replies say "on worklet".** Trace → Kind **Models**, Type contains `live.reply`: one line for each reply, such as "her voice: 4.2 s played · started after 12 ms · 0 holes · on worklet". "on buffers" means the worklet player couldn't load and the old player stood in. If you see it, write it down and tap Settings → Copy diagnostics.
- [ ] **P3. No clicks over five replies.** Ask five things, one of them long ("Tell me the whole story of Bixby Bridge, at length"). Listen for a click or a tick anywhere: mid-sentence, at the start of a reply, between sentences. Then do it again with AirPods. Should be: none. If you hear one, note roughly when; Claude matches it to its `live.reply` line.
- [ ] **P4. A tap over her stops her silently.** During a long reply, tap Shiori. She stops at once with no click (a 12 ms fade, too short to hear as a fade). The conversation shows her line cut off, ending in ✂. Trace: its `live.reply` line has "· cut ·".
- [ ] **P5. An immediate note from Kokoro stops her silently.** Ask something with a fact Kokoro may correct ("When was Bixby Bridge built, and how long is it?"). If Kokoro corrects her mid-sentence, the cut is silent, with no click, and she goes on with the correction. Trace → Kind **Halves**: a "Kokoro to Koe · immediate · …" line; Kind **Models**, `live.reply`: the cut reply has "· cut ·". The field's one recorded pop was exactly this (04:23:05 in your trace of 2026-10-22). If Kokoro never interrupts after a few tries, mark it "not provoked".
- [ ] **P6. Holes are 0, or they fade.** On Wi-Fi, the `live.reply` lines should mostly say "0 holes". Then go somewhere the signal is weak (or turn Wi-Fi off on poor cellular) and ask for a long answer. Any line saying "N holes, M ms" is a moment the sound ran dry: listen for whether those were soft (a fade out and in) and never a click. Write down the hole counts.
- [ ] **P7. The start delay (`waitMs`).** In the same lines, "started after N ms" is how long her first sound waited in the buffer: it should be under about 250 ms (the player starts at 150 ms buffered, or at 250 ms whatever it has), and usually much less, because Gemini sends the start of a reply in a burst. A delay you hear before she speaks is mostly the model thinking, not this. Write down the largest you see.
- [ ] **P8. No pop as a session opens or closes.** With no music playing, tap Shiori: listen as the chip turns **Listening live**. Say nothing for 30 seconds: listen as the chip goes back to **Voice on · Realtime** (the session closes, and the sound switches back to playback). Then the same with AirPods. Should be: no pop either way. Trace: "live session closed · Gemini 3.8 Live · … · quiet · …".
- [ ] **P9. Never stalled.** None of the `live.reply` lines from P1 to P8 has "stalled" (her sound stopped coming, and annai stopped the player). If one does, write down what you were doing just then (switching apps, a call coming in).
- [ ] **P10. Classic's streamed voice.** Tap the chip, choose **Classic**, with Gemini 3.8 Flash Lite TTS first in Speech out (it streams through the same player). Ask for a long answer: no clicks between sentences or within them. Gemini's free voice allows 10 requests a day, so after a few sentences the next voice in the list may speak: Trace → Kind **Web** shows which one each sentence used.

## The font check

The localization design (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-localization-design.md`, "Fonts") says annai's font list names Hiragino Sans before any Chinese font, and nothing marks a line's language, so on an iPhone a Chinese line is likely drawn with Japanese glyph shapes, and a simplified character that a Japanese font lacks taken from another font mid-sentence. This check confirms what happens today; the localization work (L3) will fix it.

Type these two messages to Shiori, so both lines are in the same conversation:
- Chinese: `直角三角形的骨架，这是我们说的。`
- Japanese: `直角三角形の骨組みです。`

Compare 直, 骨 and 角 in your two bubbles. Each character is drawn differently in Japanese and in simplified Chinese:

| | Japanese shape | Simplified Chinese shape |
|---|---|---|
| 直 | A separate stroke runs down the left side and turns along the bottom, like an L the box sits in; the box has 2 lines inside (3 spaces) | No stroke down the left; the box stands on one flat line; the box has 3 lines inside (4 spaces) |
| 骨 | In the top part, the small inner box sits in the right half | The small inner box sits in the left half |
| 角 | The middle stroke of the lower part stops at the lowest crossbar | The middle stroke runs right through to the bottom |

- [ ] **F1. Which shapes the Chinese line has.** Should be today: both lines show the Japanese shapes. Write down what the Chinese line shows. If the Chinese line already has the Chinese shapes, say so: the design's assumption is wrong for this iPhone.
- [ ] **F2. A character from another font.** In the Chinese line, look at 这, 们 and 说 (simplified characters a Japanese font lacks). Do they look a different weight, size or style from the characters beside them? Write down which.
- [ ] **F3. The font in use (optional, at the Mac).** Web Inspector → Elements → select the Chinese bubble's text → the Font panel: write down the font used ("Hiragino Sans" expected today, "PingFang SC" after L3).
- [ ] **F4. Android.** The same two lines on the Android phone. Android picks its fallback font by the phone's language, so write down the phone's language and which shapes each line shows.

## Doze (Android)

An idle Android phone holds back most network traffic and wakes it in short windows. annai asks for its pushes to be sent with high urgency, which Android delivers through Doze, and with an hour's time to live: a push the phone hasn't taken within the hour is dropped. A plan's "coming up" notification says the minutes left when the phone shows it, so one held back 4 minutes says "In 11 min" rather than "In 15 min", and "Now" or "Started N min ago" once the item has begun. On the emulator, one came 4 minutes late (fixed by 4a648d5); whether Doze holds them back on a real phone is this check.

Set up on the Android phone, in the evening: notifications on; a fixed plan item 1 h 30 min ahead, with no place (its "coming up" is due at 1 h 15 min); and ask Shiori "Remind me in 70 minutes to check the oven". Settings → Notifications: "On · 2 reminders waiting at the relay." Swipe annai away, unplug the phone, screen off, flat on a table, and don't touch it.

- [ ] **Z1. A reminder after an idle hour.** Shiori's reminder arrives about 30 seconds after its time, with the trip's name and her words. Write down how many minutes late it was by the notification's time. Up to a minute is fine; more is Doze holding it.
- [ ] **Z2. The "coming up" after an idle hour and a quarter.** The plan item's notification arrives; write down how late it was.
- [ ] **Z3. The text says the right "In N min".** It says "In 15 min: …" if on time, or 15 minus the minutes it was late ("In 11 min: …"). If it came after the item's start: "Now: …" or "Started N min ago: …". Never a stale "In 15 min" on a late one.
- [ ] **Z4. The Trace.** Open annai: Trace → Kind **Notifications**: "notification shown · …" for each, at the time it showed. Claude compares that with when each was due (it can also read `adb shell dumpsys notification` for the post times).
- [ ] **Z5. Forced Doze (Claude, over adb).** Claude runs `dumpsys battery unplug` and `dumpsys deviceidle force-idle`, sets a reminder 10 minutes ahead, and checks that it still arrives on time and says the right words; then `dumpsys deviceidle unforce` and `dumpsys battery reset`. Also note Chrome's battery setting (Settings → Apps → Chrome → Battery: Unrestricted, Optimized or Restricted), since it changes what Doze holds back.
- [ ] **Z6. Longer than the hour.** If a reminder never came at all, check whether the phone was idle more than an hour past its time: the push service drops it then, by design. Write down what you saw.

## The car

`device-checklist-m3.md`'s "Voice modes and the car" section has the car basics (m3 #21, #23, #24, #26, #29 in the plan's D4), written before Realtime. Do those as D4 says, and these too: they're the Realtime and phase 2 parts. Parked for setting up; while driving, hands-free only, or a passenger taps.

**What a web app can and can't do with CarPlay and Android Auto.** CarPlay and Android Auto show only apps that Apple and Google approve for the car; a web app can't have an icon or a screen there. So annai never shows on the car's screen, and Siri or Google Assistant can't drive it. What does work: annai on the phone in its holder, her voice through the car's speakers and your voice through the car's microphone (both over CarPlay's or Android Auto's audio, as over Bluetooth), and maybe the steering wheel's media buttons (C6). Maps on the car's screen and annai on the phone go side by side.

- [ ] **C1. The car is recognized.** Voice on Auto. Connect to the car: the chip says **Voice on · car audio · Realtime**. Trace → Kind **Models**, Type contains `voice.route`: "audio route car · the microphone in use: <the car's name> · <devices>". If the chip says anything else, write down the names in that line: annai knows a car by its name (CarPlay, a make, "car", "hands-free"…), and an unknown name counts as unknown.
- [ ] **C2. Her voice and your microphone in Realtime.** Tap Shiori and talk for three or four turns. Her voice comes through the car's speakers, loud enough over road noise; your words are heard through the car's microphone (Trace → Type contains `heard`: the words are right). Note that while **Listening live** shows, the car is in phone-call mode: the thin sound is expected then.
- [ ] **C3. Music during and after.** With music playing, have a Realtime exchange: the music pauses (it isn't ducked under her). After 30 seconds of quiet, the session closes: does the music come back by itself, at full quality? In Classic: the music pauses while she speaks and comes back after (or stays paused: write down which).
- [ ] **C4. Pops through the car.** Over five replies through the car's speakers, any click? Trace → `live.session`: the ready line's latencies (Bluetooth adds output latency on Android; write it down).
- [ ] **C5. Speaking over her.** With car audio, "Cut her off by speaking" (Settings → Voice, on by default) lets you stop her by talking. Does her own voice from the car's speakers ever cut her off? If it does, turn the setting off and check a tap still stops her. A tap on Shiori always stops her.
- [ ] **C6. Steering-wheel buttons.** Voice on, after she has spoken once: press play/pause, then next track, then previous track on the wheel.
