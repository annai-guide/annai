# The glossary, for reading through

annai's names for its screens and things in Japanese (ja), Chinese, Simplified (zh-Hans) and Chinese, Traditional with Taiwan's words (zh-Hant), with the names iPhone and Android use for their own settings, and what is never translated. Every draft follows it; Shiori's glossary line is made from it.

This page is a review aid. The source is [`glossary.tsv`](glossary.tsv): one row per term, with where the English shows on screen and a note on why each word was chosen. Rows are in this order of kind (screen, thing, mode, platform, never), then by English. Its English is matched as written, so `Voice` (the screen) and `voice` (one she speaks with) are two rows. Some terms are also ordinary words (Now, Plan, Add, Ask, Allow, Share, Main, key, card): the strings check should take them as names only where a message uses them as one.

120 rows: 31 screen, 38 thing, 7 mode, 28 platform, 16 never.

## Traditional Chinese (zh-Hant)

Taiwan's words, one Traditional catalog for Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau phones (the user's choice, 2026-10-28). Each follows the zh-Hans pick where Taiwan says it the same way, and Taiwan's word where it doesn't: 檔案 (Files), 外掛程式 (Plugins), 供應商 (provider), 金鑰 (key), 匯出 (export), 封存 (Archive), 推播 (push), 即時 (realtime), 辨識 (recognition), 底部列 (dock), 開發人員 (Developer), 新增 (Add), 密碼片語 (passphrase). The phones' own words are Taiwan's iPhone's, checked on Apple's zh-tw iPhone User Guide on 2026-10-28: 加入主畫面, 分享, 主畫面, 隱私權與安全性, 定位服務, 下次詢問, 頁面選單, 設定 → App → Safari, 清除 iPhone, and 按住 for touch and hold. Apple's zh-tw guide writes Safari without 瀏覽器.

## How the platform words were checked

On 2026-10-23, from Apple's iPhone User Guide in ja-jp and zh-cn (support.apple.com, the iOS 27 pages: bookmarks and the Home Screen, web apps, location, notifications, Safari settings, hardware access, and the guide's contents for app names), and Google's Android and Chrome help in ja and zh-Hans (app permissions, notifications, web apps, website shortcuts, camera and microphone). Google marks its ja and zh pages as possibly machine-translated. Where only community answers or how-to sites agreed, the note says so.
